In South Africa, WhatsApp is often the front door. Customers would rather send a message than fill in a form, and your team replies from their own phones because it's quick.

It works until it doesn't. Then the problems arrive together: several phones, several people, no shared history. A quote sent from one phone is invisible to the rest of the team. When someone is on leave, their customers go quiet. Your CRM, the place you meant to track every deal, shows only half the picture.

The answer isn't to move customers off WhatsApp. It's to connect WhatsApp to the systems you run the business on.

## The signs it's time

- Customers message more than one person, because they're not sure who to ask.
- Nobody can say how many WhatsApp enquiries came in last week.
- Quotes and order details live in chats, not in your CRM.
- Follow-ups depend on someone remembering to scroll back.
- A staff member leaves and takes their customer conversations with them.

If two or more of these sound familiar, WhatsApp has outgrown the phone in your pocket.

## What "connected" looks like

**One business number, shared by the team.** With the WhatsApp Business API, the conversation belongs to the business, not to someone's personal phone. Several people can work the same inbox, and history stays put when someone leaves.

**Every enquiry becomes a record.** A new message creates or updates a contact in your CRM, tagged with its source. Sales can see every conversation alongside the deal.

**Routing, not guessing.** Messages go to the right person based on what's being asked: sales, support, accounts. Nobody has to decide by hand who picks it up.

**Follow-ups that run on their own.** A quote sent on WhatsApp can trigger a reminder in the CRM. If the customer goes quiet, the right person is prompted, or a polite nudge goes out automatically.

**Reporting you can trust.** Once conversations are linked to records, you can see volumes, response times and outcomes, without counting chats by hand.

## Where AI fits, and where it doesn't

Once WhatsApp is connected, an AI agent can take the first step: answer common questions, qualify the enquiry and pass it to the right person. That only works if the agent knows when to stop.

For one premium café and bistro brand, we built a WhatsApp concierge grounded in the brand's own handbooks. It never guesses about the menu, and it hands allergen and sensitive questions straight back to the team. That's the standard to aim for: fast where it's safe, human where it matters.

For field teams the same idea works internally. Appi, our WhatsApp AI teammate, puts one number in front of field agents to answer their questions, file their requests and keep their records current.

## How to approach it

1. **Map the conversations.** What do customers actually ask on WhatsApp? Which questions lead to revenue, and which are repeats?
2. **Decide where data should live.** Usually the CRM is the source of truth, and WhatsApp feeds it.
3. **Connect, then automate.** Get messages and records syncing reliably before you add routing, reminders or AI.
4. **Keep the tone.** Automated messages should sound like your business, not like a system.

Connecting WhatsApp isn't a technology project for its own sake. It's how you stop losing the enquiries you've already earned.
