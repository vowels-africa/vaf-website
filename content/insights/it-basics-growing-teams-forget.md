In a small team, IT runs on goodwill. Someone sets up the laptops, someone knows the Wi-Fi password, and everyone shares a few logins. It works until the team grows.

Then the cracks appear. A new hire waits days for access. A former employee still has the shared drive. Nobody knows which devices the business owns, or who to call when the internet goes down. None of it is dramatic, until it is.

Managed IT is the unglamorous work that prevents this. Here's what growing teams most often forget.

## 1. Onboarding that's ready on day one

A new hire's first day says a lot about a business. If they spend it waiting for an email account, a laptop and access to the right folders, they've learned something about how you run.

Write down what each role needs: accounts, tools, shared folders and devices. Then make setup a standard process, finished before the person arrives.

## 2. Offboarding that actually closes the door

This is the one most often skipped, and the riskiest. When someone leaves, their access should end the same day: email, shared drives, business tools and anything that holds customer data.

Keep a single list of what each person has access to, so offboarding is a checklist, not a memory test.

## 3. Access that matches the role

As teams grow, access tends to only grow too. People are added to systems and rarely removed. Review who can see what, especially financial and customer data, and keep it to what each role needs.

Shared logins are a common weak point. Where you can, give each person their own account.

## 4. Knowing what devices you own

Laptops, phones and other equipment are business assets, and they hold business data. Keep an up-to-date inventory: what you own, who has it and its condition. It makes onboarding faster, offboarding cleaner and replacements predictable.

## 5. Cloud and shared drive structure

Files scattered across personal drives, email attachments and old folders slow everyone down. Agree a simple structure for your shared drive, set permissions by team and make it the default place work lives.

## 6. One owner for vendors and connectivity

Internet providers, software licences and IT suppliers all need managing. When something breaks, someone should know who to call and what's in the contract. Without an owner, every outage becomes a scramble.

## What good looks like

When the basics are handled:

- New hires are productive from day one.
- People who leave can't get back in.
- Access is deliberate, not accidental.
- Devices and licences are accounted for.
- Problems go to someone who knows how to fix them.

In short, IT becomes predictable.
