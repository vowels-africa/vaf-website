Most businesses don't need more software. They need fewer things done by hand. When growth arrives, admin grows faster than headcount, and the same people who should be selling, serving or building end up copying, chasing and re-keying.

Automation fixes that, but only if you start in the right place. Our rule is simple: if it's repeated daily, it should be automated. Here are the five places we look first.

### 1. Lead capture and first response

A lead that waits goes cold. Enquiries arrive through web forms, email and WhatsApp, and someone has to notice them, type them into the CRM and reply. That's three chances for a lead to slip.

Automate the capture so every enquiry lands in one place with its source attached. Then automate the first response, so the customer hears back in minutes, not when someone is free. The conversation can stay human. The waiting shouldn't.

### 2. Follow-ups

Follow-up is where deals are lost quietly. Nobody decides to drop a prospect. It just slides down the list.

Set rules your CRM can act on. If a quote has had no reply after a set number of days, send a reminder. If a deal hasn't moved, flag it to its owner. Your team still decides what to say. The system makes sure nobody forgets to say it.

### 3. Invoices and payment reminders

Chasing payment by hand is slow and awkward, and it's often the founder doing it. Generating invoices when work is approved, and sending polite, consistent reminders on a schedule, takes the awkwardness out and keeps cash moving.

The rules are easy to write down: when to invoice, when to remind, when to escalate to a person. If you can write them down, they can run on their own.

### 4. Reporting

If someone spends Monday morning pulling numbers from three systems into a spreadsheet, that report should build itself. Connect the sources once, and the dashboard is ready when the team arrives.

The bonus is trust. A report built by hand is only as reliable as the person building it. An automated one is the same every week.

### 5. Internal approvals

Leave requests, purchase approvals, discounts and refunds. These usually live in someone's inbox, waiting. A simple approval flow routes each request to the right person, records the decision and tells everyone involved. Nothing is lost in a thread.

## How to start without breaking anything

**Fix the process first.** Automating a messy process just makes the mess faster. Remove duplicate steps and unclear hand-offs before anything is automated.

**Start with one workflow.** Pick the task that costs the most hours or loses the most money, automate it well and measure it. Then do the next one.

**Use what you already have.** Most businesses already own the tools they need. The work is connecting them properly. We build most automations with n8n, alongside the CRM, inbox and accounting system you already use.

**Keep people where they matter.** Automation should handle the repetition, not the judgement. A good test: if a mistake would embarrass you in front of a customer, a person should check it.

## What it adds up to

Across our clients, automating the repeated work gives teams back an average of 25 hours a week. That's more than half a working week returned to selling, serving and building.

You don't need to automate everything at once. You need to start with the right five.
