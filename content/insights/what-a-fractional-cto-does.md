Most growing businesses reach a point where technology decisions get bigger than anyone in the room. Which system should we standardise on? Is this developer quote reasonable? Should we build this or buy it? Can we trust the agency's timeline?

A full-time CTO would answer those questions, but many businesses don't need one yet, and can't justify the cost. What they need is someone senior in their corner, part of the time. That's a fractional CTO.

## What the role covers

**A technical sounding board.** Someone the founder or leadership team can test decisions on before money is spent. Often the most valuable conversations are short ones.

**Oversight of developers.** If you work with freelancers, an agency or a small in-house team, a fractional CTO reviews plans, checks quality and asks the questions you might not know to ask.

**Strategic prioritisation.** Growing businesses always have more technology ideas than capacity. The job is to decide what matters now, what can wait and what should never happen.

**Practical system decisions.** Choosing tools, setting standards and planning how systems should connect, so the business doesn't end up with a tangle of software nobody fully understands.

**Someone to say "don't waste money on that".** This may be the most underrated part of the job. A good fractional CTO saves money by stopping the wrong projects early.

We offer fractional CPO support too, for businesses where the product questions, what to build and for whom, matter as much as the technical ones.

## When you probably need one

- You're about to make a significant technology investment and nobody internally can judge it.
- You rely on outside developers and can't tell whether you're getting good work.
- Technology decisions keep landing on the founder, who doesn't have time for them.
- Your systems have grown piece by piece and nobody owns the overall picture.
- You're preparing for growth and want the foundations right first.

## When you probably don't

If your setup is simple, your tools work and you aren't planning major changes, you may not need ongoing senior input yet. A one-off review might be enough.

## What working together looks like

The rhythm depends on the business, but it usually includes:

- **Regular check-ins** with the founder or leadership team on priorities and decisions.
- **Reviews** of plans, quotes and delivery from developers or vendors.
- **A clear technology roadmap**, kept short and practical, and updated as the business changes.
- **Availability for the urgent questions**, so you're not making big decisions alone.

The role works best when the fractional CTO is close enough to the business to understand it, but independent enough to give an honest view.

## How to get value from it

- **Bring the decisions early.** It's cheaper to change direction before the contract is signed.
- **Be clear about authority.** Decide whether the role advises, approves or leads in each area.
- **Connect it to delivery.** Advice is most useful when someone can act on it. At Vowels, the same team can also implement: automation, integration, custom software and AI.

## The point

Not every business needs a full-time CTO. Every growing business needs someone senior in its corner on technology. A fractional CTO gives you that judgement when you need it, at a scale that fits.
